TO THE MACHINE OPERATOR

attempting to use this machine.

2.  Always wear proper eye protection.

3.  Never operate machine unless all guards are in place.

4.  Never insert hands or arms into or near cutting area while machine is running.

5.  Never load or unload machine while blade is running.

6.  Never wear loose clothing, long sleeves, gloves, jewelry, or any other items that may
be caught.  Confine long hair.

7.  Adequately support stock on both sides of machine to prevent falling.

8.  Always disconnect power at source when performing maintenance or making
adjustments, other than those necessary for the normal operation of machine.

9.  Keep cutting area clear of tools or other loose objects.

10.  Before starting a sawing cycle, be sure BOTH vises are securely clamped and
machine set-up is correct.

11.  Never use this machine to cut workpieces larger than the stated capacity.

12.  Never modify a blade to fit arbor.

13.  Never use a blade larger than the machine is designed for.

14.  When cutting very short pieces, make sure they do not jam between the blade and
the slot in the tabletop.

15.  Be aware that the pneumatic vises close automatically and to KEEP HANDS
CLEAR!   (Model C315 AV only)

CLUTCH
The clutch of your machine is extremely
important in terms of blade life, blade
protection, and transmission of power to
your workpiece.
Prior to making a single cut and
before putting on a blade, you should
remove the clutch assembly, clean
thoroughly, and apply a light coating
of oil.

If the blade stops easily in the material during the course of sawing, it may be necessary to adjust
the clutch by retightening spindle bolt, or your clutch may need to be removed and cleaned.   It is
also very  important to make sure that the problem is not a misapplication or dull blade causing
the problem.  A dull blade or a blade not properly suited for the application may also easily stall in
the material.

ANGLE CUTTING
Your machine will make angle cuts from 0-45° left or right.  It will be necessary to readjust the
vise jaw inserts on the moving vise jaws when miter cutting, as failure to do so will result in
interference with the blade.  First loosen locking lever (#2), and rotate saw frame in the desired
direction until the pointer coincides with the angle desired.  Relock lever.

VISE DOVETAIL
It is important to notice that the entire vise assembly will move laterally on a dovetail. (closer to
the sawframe pivot or closer to the saw operator)  This is adjusted by unlocking lever (#2) and
sliding the assembly to the most appropriate position;  closer to the pivot, or closer to operator.

The most advantageous position is normally so that the sawblade will going through the smallest
possible cross section at any time. This may not always be the position that allows you to get
through larger workpieces.  Examples below show the INORRECT and CORRECT positions.
If sawing small solid materials, place the center of the workpiece slightly behind spindle center.

Blade Selection

It is recommended in all cases to use the smallest sawblade possible at all times.
This will enhance the machines ability to cut, it will make it easier for the machine operator to cut,
and it will increase the life of your machine.  In addition, you may consider the blade to be similar
to a pulley;  the smaller the diameter, the greater the torque.  Normally smaller blades will cut with
more power, less vibration, and you can cut with greater ease.

The 14” sawblade is intended only for occasional maximum capacity purposes, and never
intended to be used for production.  Extended use of 14” sawblades will decrease gearbox
life.

Blade Spindle Speeds

Special Sawblade Grinds

All standard blades are ground for use on mild steels.

Blades to be used on stainless steels (or other hard materials) should be designated with an “SS”
after the part number (ex:  1204 SS)  These blades may also be used on mild steels.

Blades to be used on aluminum or other non-ferrous materials should be designated with an “AL
after the part number (ex: 1210 AL)  These blades may not be used on steels.

VISE
The vise is constructed to slide on prismatic ways which are adjustable for wear.  By loosening the lock nuts along the
sides and adjusting equally on each of the studs you may take up for wear.  Note that you should position the vise nut
adjacent to each of the adjustment points before adjusting.  Do not overtighten, as the vise will then work with great
difficulty and excessive wear will occur.

TROUBLESHOOTING

Aside from obvious machine malfunctions, most sawing problems can be attributed to improper practices or applications,
and will result in blade problems.

1.  Improper application, i.e. an application not suited to a circular sawing machine
2.  Improper blade selection, including

-wrong diameter

-wrong pitch

-wrong grind

-dull blade
3.  Saw blade not properly mounted on clean spindle
4.  Material improperly clamped in machine vise
5.  Improper use of coolant
6.   Feed rate too heavy
7.  Improper machine maintenance, especially of spindle, flanges, or vises
8.  Improper training of saw operator

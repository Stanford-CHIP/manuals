# Printable HTV roles — clear vs opaque, masks, color charts

## Synopsis

Lab conversation (follow up with Ash) on BN-series printable heat transfer: how Siser TTD and Roland PTM2 relate to printable films (ColorPrint / HTMS / HTM3), when clear film is useful vs opaque white film, what “light fabric” means, buying a mainstream clear eco-solvent roll, and whether printing a color chart onto HTV is worthwhile.

## Conclusions

- **Two product roles:** printable HTV (film that goes on the printer/cutter) vs transfer mask (tape that lifts weeded graphics for pressing). TTD is Siser’s mask line, not a clear version of HTMS. PTM2 is Roland’s mask for HeatSoft films.
  - Mask role: [web/siser-ttd-masks.md → ## Overview], [web/roland-pgm-ptm2-mask.md → ## Overview]
  - Printable films: [web/roland-heatsoft-satin-htms.md → ## Overview], [web/roland-heatsoft-plus-htm3.md → ## Overview], [uscutter_siser_material_types.md, Page 1]

- **Siser ColorPrint workflow** uses print → cut → weed → mask with TTD Easy and/or High Tack (product-dependent), then heat press. Soft Opaque calls for High Tack; Sublithin for Easy Mask. [uscutter_siser_material_types.md, Page 1]

- **HTMS / HTM3 are opaque printable films** (~90% opacity; HTMS marketed for dark fabrics / high white point). They are not clear/transparent carrier films. [web/roland-heatsoft-satin-htms.md → ## Features], [web/roland-heatsoft-satin-htms.md → ## Specifications], [web/roland-heatsoft-plus-htm3.md → ## Specifications]

- **Clear printable HTV** (e.g. Stahls SuperTEK Matte Clear) is for **white or light fabrics only**, solvent/eco-solvent print & cut, typically **no mask**, mirror print on adhesive side. It is not a substitute for opaque film on dark shirts. [web/stahls-supertek-matte-clear.md → ## Key constraints], [web/stahls-supertek-matte-clear.md → ## Print / cut / press (summary)]

- **“Light fabric”** means white / cream / pale pastels — not standard red, navy, black, etc. On mid/dark shirts, use opaque printable HTV; use cutouts/weeding for “transparent” composition (shirt shows through holes), not clear film.

- **Mainstream clear roll to buy (US):** Stahls CAD-COLOR SuperTEK Matte Clear, 20″ starter lengths via McLogan (or Stahls direct). Example retail snapshot: 5 / 10 / 25 yd × 20″. [web/mclogan-supertek-matte-clear-buy.md → ## Variants listed], [vinyl-printer-cutter/manifest.yaml → mclogan-supertek-matte-clear]

- **Color chart on HTV:** Yes, when you care how colors look **after print + press on that media** (and ideally on the shirt color you’ll use). Prefer a small swatch strip. Use **opaque** HTV for charts meant for dark garments; **clear** only for light-shirt appearance. For RIP/ink calibration alone, cheaper vinyl/paper is enough — use HTV when media/hand/sheen matter.

- **Dark shirts + soft translucent fades** are outside standard clear HTV; opaque print-and-cut or other processes (e.g. white underbase / DTF) apply. BN2-30 can be configured CMYK+White for specialty media, but clear apparel HTV remains specified for light fabrics in the frozen Stahls/McLogan sources above.
